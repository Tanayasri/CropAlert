The problem

Spotting a disease is hard. Finding trustworthy advice is harder.

CropAlert was built around a simple frustration: when a farmer notices something wrong with a field, the answers are scattered — across forums, videos, extension bulletins and books — and many of them conflict. Meanwhile the disease keeps spreading.

Symptoms overlap

Four different rice diseases can all look like “yellowing leaves” at a glance, yet each needs a different response.

Advice is scattered

Growers waste hours comparing disconnected sources, unsure which recommendation applies to their crop and symptoms.

Every day counts

Fast-moving diseases such as late blight can cross a field within days, so a delay in identification quickly becomes a loss in yield.

Experts are scarce

Extension officers cover large areas, so a first structured check can bridge the gap until a specialist is available.

How it works

Four steps, about two minutes

CropAlert asks the same questions an agronomist would ask first — what you can see, where it appears, and how it is spreading — then matches your answers against the four diseases known for that crop.

  1. 1

    Select your crop

    Choose rice, tomato or potato so the right disease set is loaded.

  2. 2

    Answer symptom questions

    Six multiple-choice questions about lesions, spread, weather and plant parts. You can go back and change any answer.

  3. 3

    CropAlert analyzes your responses

    Each answer is scored against every candidate disease to build a ranked prediction.

  4. 4

    Receive a possible disease prediction and useful information

    See the most likely disease, a confidence score, the answers that drove the result, plus management and prevention guidance.

Important: preliminary educational tool

CropAlert provides a preliminary assessment for educational and informational purposes. It is not a substitute for professional agricultural diagnosis or advice.

The prediction is produced by a transparent scoring prototype running in your browser — it is not a trained, field-validated model, and it does not examine images, lab samples or field history. Always confirm a result with an agronomist or extension officer before applying any treatment, and follow the product label for any pesticide you use.
