About

About CropAlert

CropAlert is a student-developed prototype that turns a handful of symptom questions into a structured, explainable first opinion about crop disease — in about two minutes, on any phone or laptop, without uploading a single photo.

What CropAlert is

CropAlert is a symptom-based crop disease assessment tool. You select your crop, answer six multiple-choice questions about what you can observe — the shape and colour of lesions, where they appear, how quickly they spread, the recent weather — and CropAlert ranks the four diseases it knows for that crop.

The result is deliberately presented as a preliminary assessment: a most likely disease, a confidence percentage, the specific answers that drove the prediction, and the reference information an agronomist would want you to know — causal organism, affected plant parts, favourable conditions, management and prevention.

This version does not accept image input. It is designed as the conversation that happens before a photo or a lab sample: gather the symptoms systematically, narrow the options, and know what to look at next.

The problem it is solving

When a grower sees an unhealthy field, the information they need exists — but it is scattered across extension leaflets, search results, videos, forums and experience, in different languages and levels of detail.

Conflicting sources

Two search results can recommend opposite treatments for the same symptoms, because they are describing two different diseases that look similar in the early stages.

No structured starting point

Most growers are not asked the diagnostic questions that actually separate one disease from another, so they never narrow the field of possibilities.

Specialists are not on call

Extension officers cover large areas. A grower may wait days for a visit while the disease completes another infection cycle.

Why early identification matters

Most crop diseases are far easier and cheaper to hold back when they are caught in the first few days than after they have spread through a field. Identification timing changes the whole economics of the response.

  • Lower cost per action. Spot treatments and a single well-timed spray are far cheaper than whole-field rescue applications.
  • Fewer infection cycles. Rapidly spreading diseases such as late blight can cover a field in a matter of days under the right weather.
  • The right product, used correctly. Fungicides do not cure bacterial or viral diseases, so a wrong guess wastes money and adds unnecessary chemical load.
  • Protected neighbours. Stopping one outbreak early reduces inoculum and vector pressure for every field around it.

Who CropAlert is for

CropAlert is a first-pass tool for anyone who needs a structured starting point before a specialist is involved.

Smallholder farmers

A quick, free check that works on a phone and produces questions worth asking an agronomist.

Extension workers

A consistent way to structure a first interview in the field and to explain the options to growers.

Students & educators

A transparent example of how symptom data becomes a prediction — every score can be inspected.

Home gardeners

Clear prevention and management notes for the most common tomato and potato problems.

Why CropAlert instead of searching manually

CropAlert does not replace expertise — it replaces the twenty scattered tabs you would otherwise open first.

Searching across sources

  • —Results depend on knowing the right keywords for a disease you may not have identified yet.
  • —Advice is often for another crop, climate or growth stage.
  • —Contradictory recommendations with no way to compare them.
  • —No record of what you observed or decided.
  • —Minutes to hours per problem, repeated every season.

Using CropAlert

  • +Starts from what you can physically see, so no terminology is needed.
  • +Only asks about the twelve diseases actually covered for your crop.
  • +One ranked result with a confidence score and the answers behind it.
  • +Reference information kept in a searchable Disease Library.
  • +Consistent output every time — the same answers give the same result.

The AI / ML component

How the prediction works, at a simple level

Think of it as teaching a program to ask the same question an experienced agronomist asks: “which disease fits these symptoms best?”

  1. Step 1

    Turn symptoms into numbers

    Each answer becomes a feature — a number that describes one observation, such as “lesions are spindle-shaped”.

  2. Step 2

    Score every candidate

    Features are combined into a score for each of the four diseases of the crop. Strong evidence adds more than weak evidence; contradictory evidence subtracts.

  3. Step 3

    Rank and convert to confidence

    Scores are normalised into percentages. The confidence blends how dominant the top score is with how much of the available evidence was captured.

  4. Step 4

    Explain the result

    The answers that contributed most are listed next to the prediction, so the output can be checked rather than blindly trusted.

Honest status of this component

In this release the scoring runs in your browser as a transparent weighting prototype. It is not a trained deep-learning or scikit-learn model, it has not been evaluated against labelled field data, and CropAlert makes no claim of diagnostic accuracy. The scoring function is written so it can be swapped for a real trained model later — the same input (crop and answers) and the same output (ranked diseases with probabilities) — without changing the interface.

Current scope: 3 crops and 12 diseases

CropAlert deliberately stays small. Four diseases per crop keeps the questions discriminating and the explanation readable. Anything outside this list is out of scope — the tool will still return a result, but it can only ever choose from the twelve diseases below.

Rice (4)

  • Rice Blast
  • Rice Sheath Blight
  • Rice Bacterial Leaf Blight
  • Rice Tungro

Tomato (4)

  • Tomato Early Blight

  • Tomato Bacterial Spot
  • Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot

Student project

This is a student-developed project

CropAlert was built as an academic software project to practise the full path of a real-world product: researching a domain problem, modelling disease information, designing an accessible multi-page interface, and implementing a prediction pipeline that can later be connected to a trained model.

It is not a commercial product, it has not been reviewed or endorsed by any agricultural authority, and it has not been validated against field data. Disease descriptions were compiled from standard plant pathology references for educational use; always follow local extension recommendations and pesticide labels.

Limitations of AI-based disease assessment

CropAlert provides a preliminary assessment for educational and informational purposes. It is not a substitute for professional agricultural diagnosis or advice.

  • • The prediction is only as good as the answers entered; subjective or rushed answers reduce reliability.
  • • Several diseases share symptoms, and mixed or secondary infections are possible — only one “most likely” disease is shown.
  • • The tool covers 12 diseases in 3 crops and cannot identify anything else, nutritional disorders, herbicide damage or pests.
  • • It does not inspect images, soil, weather data or field history, and its confidence figure is a score, not a laboratory probability.
  • • No diagnosed accuracy rate has been established for this prototype.

Confirm results with an agronomist, extension officer or diagnostic laboratory before deciding on any control measure.
