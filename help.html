Help

How to use CropAlert

The whole assessment takes about two minutes. You do not need an account, you do not need to upload photos, and nothing you enter leaves your device.

Start Assessment

Five steps

Follow these steps on the App page.

  1. 1

    Select a crop

    Choose Rice, Tomato or Potato. Only the four diseases relevant to that crop are considered from that point on.

  2. 2

    Answer the symptom questions

    Six questions, one answer each. Look at the affected plants first, then pick the option that matches best. The progress bar shows where you are, and Back lets you change any earlier answer.

  3. 3

    Submit the assessment

    On the last question the button reads See my result. CropAlert cannot continue while a question is unanswered — you will see a clear message instead.

  4. 4

    Review the predicted disease

    Check the most likely disease, the confidence percentage, the answers that contributed, and how the other three diseases compared. Save the result if you want to keep it on this device.

  5. 5

    Read the management and prevention information

    The result card includes the causal organism, affected parts, favourable conditions, management and prevention — plus a link to the full profile in the Disease Library.

  6. ✓

    Then confirm with a person

    Share the prediction and your observations with an agronomist or extension officer before applying any treatment. CropAlert prepares the conversation — it does not end it.

What CropAlert can and cannot do

Knowing the boundaries of a tool is part of using it well.

CropAlert can

  • +Structure your observations into consistent diagnostic questions.
  • +Rank four candidate diseases for rice, tomato or potato and show how close they were.
  • +Explain which of your answers drove the prediction.
  • +Give you reference information on symptoms, causes, favourable conditions, management and prevention.
  • +Produce the same result for the same answers, every time.
  • +Run entirely in your browser, so your answers stay on your device.

CropAlert cannot

  • −Identify diseases other than the twelve it covers, or detect pests, weeds, nutrient or herbicide problems.
  • −Accept photos or samples in this version — it works from your answers alone.
  • −Recognise mixed infections: only one “most likely” disease is returned.
  • −Replace a laboratory test, a field visit or an agronomist’s diagnosis.
  • −Guarantee a treatment outcome or claim a validated accuracy rate.
  • −Consider your field’s history, soil, variety or local weather forecast.

Understanding the prediction and confidence score

Every answer option carries a weight for each candidate disease: strong supporting evidence adds several points, weaker or overlapping evidence adds a little, and evidence that argues against a disease subtracts. CropAlert adds those weights up across your six answers to produce a score for each of the four diseases.

The disease with the highest score becomes the prediction. Two numbers are then shown:

  • Share (%) — in “How the four diseases compared”, each disease’s percentage is its slice of the total supporting score. The four always add up to about 100%.
  • Confidence (%) — the headline figure. It blends the winning disease’s share with how much of the available evidence you confirmed, and is deliberately capped at 90% so the prototype never looks more certain than it should be.

Confidence bands

The band is always written out, so the meaning never depends on colour alone.

75–90%
Strong match. Your answers fit one disease clearly — still confirm before acting.
55–74%
Moderate match. One disease leads, but some answers overlap with the others.
40–54%
Limited match. Symptoms are shared. Re-check your answers and consider the runner-up.
below 40%
Weak match — inconclusive. The answers do not point to a single disease. Consult an agronomist.

A worked example

If five of your answers strongly match Rice Blast and one answer also fits sheath blight, rice blast collects the largest score, its share of the total rises, and the confidence lands in the “strong match” band. Swap one answer for a contradictory one and both the ranking and the confidence drop.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to upload a photo of the plant?

No. This version of CropAlert does not use image input at all. You simply describe what you can see by choosing the closest matching option for each question.

How long does an assessment take?

Around two minutes: one crop selection and six questions. Walk through the field first so your answers reflect what you actually observe.

Can I change an answer after selecting it?

Yes. Use Back to return to any earlier question — your previous choice stays selected, and picking a different option updates the result immediately. Restart assessment clears everything and returns to crop selection.

What exactly does the confidence percentage mean?

It is a matching score, not a statistical probability and not a measure of how sick the plant is. It tells you how strongly your reported symptoms correspond to the leading disease relative to the other three, capped at 90%. See “Understanding the prediction and confidence score” above for the full explanation.

Is a real AI model running in my browser?

Not yet, and CropAlert does not claim otherwise. The current prediction is a prototype scoring demonstration written in JavaScript: transparent weights, no training, no validated accuracy. It is structured so a trained scikit-learn model could replace the scoring function later without changing the interface.

The result looks wrong — what should I do?

First re-check your answers, especially the questions about lesion shape and location — they carry the most weight. Then compare the runner-up diseases in the result and read their profiles in the Disease Library. If symptoms still do not match, treat the result as a starting point and get a professional diagnosis.

Are my answers uploaded anywhere?

No. The assessment runs entirely in your browser. If you press Save result, the summary is stored in your browser’s local storage on this device only, and Clear saved results removes it.

My disease is not in the list. Can CropAlert still help?

Only twelve diseases across three crops are covered. If the problem is outside that scope — or caused by pests, nutrients or chemical damage — the tool will still return the best of its four options, which may not be correct. Use the assessment as a structured starting point and seek advice beyond it.

Should I apply a pesticide straight away?

No. Fungicides do not work on bacterial or viral diseases, and bactericides do not work on fungal ones. Confirm the diagnosis first, choose a product registered for that problem and crop, and always follow the label rate and safety instructions.

Does it work on a phone, and without internet?

Yes to both — the layout adapts to small screens and the whole site is plain HTML, JavaScript and Tailwind CSS. Internet is needed only the first time (to fetch the stylesheet and fonts); after that the pages open directly from the folder, including by double-clicking index.html.
